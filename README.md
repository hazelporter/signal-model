Terrain–Map Signal Models

A philosophical tool for exploring perception, pressure, and processing

This project is an interactive set of conceptual models that explore how signals move between terrain (body, environment, lived structure) and map (meaning, philosophy, language, internal frameworks).

These models are not scientific simulations and make no empirical claims.
They are philosophical instruments — a way of describing how perception and interpretation may feel as they move through experience.

The goal is not explanation or proof, but orientation.

The Core Idea

Signals do not exist in isolation.
They move through an inner architecture shaped by alignment, pressure, and structure.

This project explores three structural states of that movement:

Corridor — Transmission
Aligned terrain and map allow signals to pass quickly with little residue.

Curvature — Digestion
Signals slow, bend, and integrate through philosophy, ethics, memory, or analogy.

Thicket — Absorption
Signals become entangled near terrain and may remain unarticulated, appearing later as pressure or symbol.

These are not fixed identities.
They are shifting modes of processing.

Philosophy of the Model

This work is best understood as:

a tool for reflection,

a philosophy of perception,

an interactive metaphor for inner processing.

It is intentionally subjective.

Rather than describing the external world scientifically, the models describe an experience of navigating meaning — how pressure reshapes pathways between sensation and interpretation.

Why GitHub?

The models are presented as small interactive sketches because movement helps reveal structure.

The code is simple by design.
It exists to support exploration, not to produce data.

How to Use

Open any model and adjust the pressure slider.

Observe how the channel changes:

fast passage

slowed transformation

entanglement near terrain

There is no correct reading.
The models are meant to be used, not solved.
